## Cost-conscious discovery and verification

1. Inventory files, byte sizes, hashes, duplicates and extraction quality by page before choosing OCR or models. Preserve immutable source files and 1-based physical PDF page locators.
2. Extract text locally. Review low-text or corrupted pages and apply OCR only where needed. Audit multilingual coverage and translations. Detect AI terminology, abbreviations and relevant contextual variants without treating generic digitalization as AI.
3. Embed only context-verified AI-related passages and the adjacent context necessary to interpret them, not entire speeches. The selected provider is OpenAI, model text-embedding-3-large, default 3072 dimensions (official documentation checked 25 September 2026). Use the Embeddings API only at the text-to-vector stage, after EN-only source selection, extraction, keyword screening and contextual verification. Keep agglomerative hierarchical clustering with cosine distance and average linkage local. Review representative and boundary passages and TF-IDF descriptive terms. Document model, dimensions, settings and limits. Clusters never allocate countries to exclusive policy camps. Reuse vectors only when model, dimensions and preprocessing match; regional regrouping alone does not invalidate them. The old MiniLM discovery outputs were removed; generate OpenAI vectors for the selected passages across all included years when execution is resumed. API credentials or model selection do not override the stop instruction; no paid API calls are authorized at this stage.
   Discovery safeguards (added after the removed MiniLM cosine/average run collapsed into one dominant cluster: 176 of 181 sampled passages at k=4 and 161 of 181 at k=12, leaving nothing interpretable for TF-IDF naming):
   - Cluster every context-verified AI passage from all included years. Do not sample one passage per country-year; a few hundred to low thousands of passages fit easily in memory. To stop long or repetitive speeches from dominating, deduplicate near-identical passages within a speech and record each speech's passage count; if one speech still contributes a disproportionate share of a candidate cluster, flag it in review.
   - Before computing cosine distances, subtract the corpus mean vector and re-normalize to unit length. This reduces the shared "UN speech about technology" direction that makes average linkage chain everything together. Record whether centering was applied.
   - Compare a small set of cuts (for example k = 6, 8, 10, 12, 16 and one cosine-distance threshold) and save cluster sizes, cosine silhouette and cophenetic correlation for each. A cut is unusable if one cluster holds more than 40% of passages or if most clusters are singletons. In that case, re-cluster the dominant cluster with the same metric and linkage, and record the split. Do not switch to Ward with cosine distance.
   - For each candidate cluster, read at least 5 representative passages (closest to the centroid) and 3 boundary passages. Compute TF-IDF on one shared vocabulary and preprocessing pipeline across all clusters. TF-IDF terms and passages together suggest a name and inclusion/exclusion rules; neither alone finalizes a theme. Review small clusters and outliers as possible minority or emerging themes; do not force them into large themes.
4. Read full AI passages and adjacent context; build a multilabel codebook grounded in those statements. Record each code decision with file, hash, page, quote, rationale and reviewer status. Review screened negatives to assess omissions. Do not convert unreviewed candidates into verified statistics.
5. Store explicit Yes/No/Uncertain/Pending decisions with source evidence. Aggregate reviewed Yes by country-year OR; assign 0 only after complete review, otherwise NA. Key Facts group denominators are reviewed Member State main addresses. Themes group denominators are AI-positive Member States with completed thematic classification. Always report n/N.
6. Compute within-speech co-mentions from the binary multilabel matrix (X transpose times X). Report n(A∩B), both directional conditional ratios n(A∩B)/n(A) and n(A∩B)/n(B), and baseline marginal frequencies; zero denominators are NA. Jaccard is optional. Co-mention is not political alignment, causation or a sentence-level link.
7. Apply every theme definition to every included AI passage using one versioned, common taxonomy across years. If discovery changes a definition, review affected historical classifications against that definition before reporting trends; never compare unharmonized codebooks. Multiple labels mean theme percentages can sum to more than 100%. Speaker rank should be completed for all reviewed addresses where metadata permits; retain unknown rather than infer rank from AI positivity.
8. The classification unit is the passage, not the country-year. Every included AI passage gets an explicit Yes/No/Uncertain/Pending value for every taxonomy code, with evidence, in paragraph_theme_labels.csv. The discovery cluster ID is stored in a separate field and never copied into a theme value. Country-year values are derived only by the OR rule in step 5. The earlier review notes in cache/source_review/ record themes per country-year with page numbers, not per passage. They may be shown to a reviewer as a consistency aid, but they cannot fill passage labels, and any disagreement is resolved by rereading the source.
9. Year-on-year theme comparison uses one taxonomy and reports n/N for every year. Show theme percentages in a trend chart or comparative sentence only for years where N, the number of AI-positive Member States with completed classification, is at least 20. Below that threshold, report counts and name the countries or cases, not percentages. On the earlier source review, this likely limits percentage comparisons to 2023 onward, but confirm it from the new classification. Mark 2019 as missing and 2018 as severely incomplete, never as zero. Distinguish change within the same countries (a matched panel of countries that are AI-positive in both compared years) from change caused by a larger set of countries mentioning AI. Mark 2026 as a partial count if the session is not complete at the cutoff.

## Emerging and recurring AI terminology by year

Alongside annual mention rates and multilabel themes, identify important AI-related keywords and multiword expressions that newly appear and recur across speeches. Discover these from the speeches rather than assuming a predetermined list is present. Verify every reported expression in its AI context; generic digital terminology and incidental acronym matches are insufficient.

- Count distinct Member States per year, once per country and expression regardless of repetitions within an address. Record both original expressions and reviewed multilingual equivalents under a transparent normalized term. Keep substantively different concepts separate.
- Distinguish first observed use, first recurring use across countries, and subsequent growth or persistence. At least two independent Member State speeches are necessary to call a term recurring within a year; always show the actual country count. A potentially important single-country innovation may be discussed separately as an isolated proposal, never as a repeated pattern. This is a descriptive screening rule, not a statistical significance test.
- Show the first observed year in the available corpus, the countries using the expression, annual n/N and the comparison years. For keyword prevalence, N is the number of AI-positive Member State addresses whose relevant passages have completed keyword review in that year. Report review coverage alongside N; do not treat pending review as non-use. Use the same fixed UN regional-group mapping for any group comparisons.
- Check important apparent new terms against earlier available original texts, including variants and multilingual equivalents. Say "first observed in the available corpus", not that a policy or term originated that year. Missing 2019, sparse 2018 coverage, incomplete 2026 and changes in source format can prevent conclusions about emergence or growth. Unknown years remain unknown, not zero.
- Prioritize terms that indicate a substantive shift in the problem raised, requested international response or expected UN function. Separate vocabulary changes from changes in policy position. Term frequency is not a theme label, endorsement or collective group position.
- Preserve a keyword evidence register with normalized term, original wording, language, country, year, source ID/file/hash, PDF page or transcript paragraph/time, excerpt and review status. Produce a yearly_keyword_trends table from reviewed evidence, with numerator, denominator, first-observed/recurrence flags and coverage caveats. Keep three layers explicit in Role of UN: institutional background from the reference documents; positions and requests established by General Debate speech evidence; and the report's own implications and recommendations. A reference document's proposal is not evidence that a country endorsed it, and background notes do not enter country-level speech counts.

Track the Global Dialogue on AI Governance, Independent International Scientific Panel on AI, and proposed Global Fund for AI Capacity Development separately, verifying their status at the report cutoff. Distinguish an explicit name from an inferred reference, and code mention, welcome, support, concrete request, reservation/concern, opposition and commitment separately. Welcome acknowledges that a mechanism exists or was established. Support backs its work or continuation. Treat them as distinct stances. A commitment is an explicit pledge or undertaking by the speaker's State (such as contributing resources, hosting, or participating); record the actor, action and any specified timing/resources with evidence. Do not equate general support, an offer, a request directed at others, a future pledge and completed delivery. Multiple stance codes are allowed for the same country. Generic support for AI cooperation is not endorsement of a specific institution. Absence of a reference is not opposition. Recommendations should explicitly identify the relevant addressee: UN, SPMU, Tech Envoy, Panel and/or Dialogue.

For FSB, IAEA and other models, verify the actual proposer and record the requested functions (for example scientific assessment, coordination, monitoring, standard-setting or verification). Do not infer an inspection or enforcement mandate from a comparison alone. Keep institutional background sources separate from General Debate evidence and statistics.

Institutional stance records (institution_stances.csv): one row per country-year-mechanism-evidence passage, with these fields:
- year, iso3, speech_id, source file/hash, page or transcript locator, quote.
- mechanism: Dialogue, Panel, Fund, FSB-like, IAEA-like, IPCC-like, CERN-like, new body/verification regime, other named model.
- reference_type: explicit name, name variant (for example "Global Fund for AI", "AI capacity-building fund") or inferred description (for example "a UN body to verify AI systems").
- stance flags (0/1, several may be 1): mention, welcome, support, request, concern, opposition, commitment.
- requested_functions: a multi-select from a fixed vocabulary of dialogue/convening, scientific assessment, capacity building, financing, norms/rules/standards, monitoring/oversight/verification, coordination, other (describe). Use an empty value when no function is stated.
- un_role: explicit, meaning the UN or a named UN mechanism is asked to do something, or generic, meaning a call for international cooperation that names no UN role. Only explicit rows count as requests to the UN.
- commitment details (actor, action, timing/resources) when commitment = 1.
- review_status and rationale.

Role of UN reports two tables built from reviewed rows. The first counts countries by mechanism and stance, and shows n and the country list. The second lists the main models with their proposer, requested functions and a short evidence quote. A country counts once per mechanism-stance cell in each year.
